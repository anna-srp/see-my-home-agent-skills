# Custom Furniture Design

Turn incomplete visual ideas into a coherent furniture concept without pretending it is engineered for manufacture.

Keep all user-facing text in English and use inches in user-facing dimensions for the United States market.

## Source authority

Accept a hand sketch, one inspiration image, a written brief, or any combination. A sketch primarily controls topology, proportions, component placement, silhouette, and viewpoint. An inspiration image primarily controls form language, material character, color, finish, and detail treatment. When both are present, use the user's stated priority; otherwise preserve sketch structure and use the inspiration image for styling.

Do not copy branding, logos, or a protected commercial design wholesale. Only explicit locked controls are hard constraints. Ask one concise question when authoritative inputs conflict in a way that changes the concept materially.

## Build the concept specification

Before generation, define:

- furniture category and subtype;
- intended use and setting;
- overall width, depth, and height when supported;
- category-correct component inventory and counts;
- material, finish, color, edge, support, upholstery, and hardware logic;
- must-keep details, editable details, and unresolved assumptions.

Preserve category anatomy: tables need a top and coherent support system; chairs need seat, back, frame, and any requested arms; sofas need module layout, cushions, arms, seams, and base; lamps need shade or diffuser, light head, stem or arms, and base, mount, or canopy. Leave unsupported dimensions open rather than inventing tolerances.

## Render and refine

Generate one isolated product concept on a clean neutral background with no people, room scene, companion furniture, text, or watermark. Use the validated specification and supplied references directly. If generation is asynchronous, wait for completion and do not duplicate a pending job.

Inspect the result for category identity, source fidelity, component count, proportions, material consistency, cropping, and obvious physical incoherence. Make at most one targeted correction before reporting a limitation.

When the user asks for drawings, create separate front, side, and top orthographic concept views from the confirmed concept. Use black linework on white, no perspective convergence, no hidden parts drawn through opaque surfaces, and no dimensions that were not confirmed. Clearly label every drawing as concept-level rather than fabrication-ready.

Deliver the render or requested views directly with the concise specification and remaining assumptions. Never claim structural capacity, electrical certification, fire performance, code compliance, joinery engineering, manufacturing tolerances, or guaranteed manufacturability.
